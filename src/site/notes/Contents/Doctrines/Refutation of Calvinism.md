# A Theological Refutation of Calvinism

## 1. Introduction: The Contemporary Rise and Strategic Challenge of Reformed Theology

In the current theological climate, the resurgence of Reformed theology—modernly known as Calvinism—has moved beyond academic circles to become a dominant "trend of the nation." This resurgence is not a harmless shift in preference; it is a strategic challenge that demands a prepared, scriptural response from the modern church. To ignore the spread of these doctrines is to leave the flock vulnerable to a system that, while cloaked in intellectualism, fundamentally distorts the character of God.

The primary motivation for this critique is the recognition that Reformed doctrine is not merely incorrect; it is "blasphemous." By teaching that the Almighty intentionally bars a significant portion of humanity from even the possibility of salvation, Calvinism slanders the goodness of God and the efficacy of the Cross. This "complete bunk" has profound regional impacts, often paralyzing evangelistic fervor and replacing the simple "whosoever will" of the Gospel with a fatalistic decree. To expose these errors, we must first look at the human foundations where these ideas are codified, beginning with the movement’s historical confessions.

---

## 2. The Foundational Conflict: Human Confessions vs. Scriptural Authority

The theological divide often starts with a "red flag": the elevation of the Westminster Confession of Faith (1646) to a status nearly equal to—and often interpretive over—the Bible. While Christianity flourished for sixteen centuries before this document, many modern Calvinists act as if the faith was only properly "penned down" in the 1600s. These human confessions, though complex, lack the foolproof clarity of the Word of God. When men try to map the mechanics of the universe through their own systems, their words inevitably begin "tripping on themselves."

Section 3.1 of the Westminster Confession provides a textbook example of this internal "double speak."

| Calvinist Claim (Westminster 3.1)                     | The Immediate Caveat                   | Logical Conflict                                                                                                                                             |
| ----------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| God "unchangeably ordained whatsoever comes to pass." | God is "neither... the author of sin." | Their words "trip on themselves." If every sinful act is unchangeably ordained by God, he is logically the source, making the caveat a hollow contradiction. |

**The "So What?" Layer** This logical inconsistency proves that human confessions are a shaky foundation. Unlike the Bible, which provides a cohesive worldview, Reformed theology requires exhausting "theological gymnastics" to maintain its systematic integrity. When a theological framework contradicts itself in its opening chapters, it reveals that it is a product of human tradition rather than divine revelation. This reliance on a "fatalistic decree" sets the stage for a skewed understanding of divine sovereignty.

---

## 3. The Sovereignty Paradox: Divine Providence vs. Human Responsibility

A major error in Reformed thinking is a false dichotomy: the idea that if God does not decree every single human action, He is not sovereign. This is narrow, finite thinking. In reality, God is so sovereign that He can "walk and chew gum at the same time"—He can be fully in charge while simultaneously decreeing that mankind possesses a genuine free will. True sovereignty is God’s power to manage a world of free agents, not His need to script every move like a puppet master.

We must distinguish between God "ordaining" sin and God "providentially" managing it. The Bible shows God taking the wicked choices of men and working them into His plans:

- **The Joseph Typology (Genesis 50:20):** Joseph’s brothers chose, of their own free will, to commit the sin of selling him into slavery. God did not force them to sin, but His providential hand was "even bigger" than their evil. He took their wicked decision and used it to save lives.
- **The Crucifixion (Acts 2:23; 4:27; John 19:11):** Herod, Pilate, and the Jews acted with "wicked hands." God knew their choices and used them for the redemption of the world, but He did not dictate their sin. Christ explicitly told Pilate he had "sin," and the Bible warns "woe to that man by whom the offense cometh." If God decreed the offense, the "woe" would be a mockery of justice.

Furthermore, even Satan is not a Calvinist. As the source of the "snatched seed" in the parable of the sower, Satan clearly believes that human response is a variable he can influence. If every outcome were unchangeably decreed, Satan's efforts to keep people from believing would be an exercise in futility.

**The "So What?" Layer** Mistaking God’s providence for a fatalistic decree distorts the nature of human responsibility. God is powerful enough to achieve His ends through the free-will decisions of His creatures. To claim otherwise is to deny the true scope of the Cross, leading directly to the problematic doctrine of Limited Atonement.

---

## 4. The "Whosoever" Doctrine: Refuting Limited Atonement and Predestination

The character of God is defined by the scope of His invitation. In the New Testament, the word "All" is a strategic battleground. If "all" does not mean "all," then the Gospel is no longer a message for the world, but a secret message for a club. The "Universal Opportunity" verses establish a clear, non-negotiable truth:

- **Romans 5:18:** The parallel is undeniable. If "all men" were condemned by Adam’s sin, then the "free gift" must likewise be available to "all men." To limit the second "all" while leaving the first "all" universal is dishonest interpretation.
- **Romans 10:13:** "For _whosoever_ shall call upon the name of the Lord shall be saved."
- **1 Timothy 2:4-6:** God "will have _all men_ to be saved" and gave Himself a "ransom for _all_."
- **2 Peter 3:9:** The Lord is "long-suffering... not willing that _any_ should perish, but that _all_ should come to repentance."
- **1 John 2:2:** Christ is the propitiation for our sins, "and not for ours only, but also for the sins of the _whole world_."
- **Hebrews 2:9:** Jesus should "taste death for _every man_."

**The "So What?" Layer** The plain reading of these texts makes "Limited Atonement" logically and biblically untenable. It is an "absurdity" to describe God as "long-suffering" and "waiting" for repentance (2 Peter 3:9) if He has already decreed that the individual is incapable of responding. Such a view implies that God commands a "false task," demanding repentance from those He has barred from the ability to repent. This flies in the face of a righteous and holy God.

---

## 5. Systematic Deconstruction of Key Calvinist Proof-Texts

A biblical worldview does not fear "scary" passages; it allows them to harmonize with the whole of Scripture. When context is applied, the favourite proof-texts of Reformed theology crumble.

### The "Drawing" Argument John 6:44 vs. John 12:32

Calvinists use John 6:44 to claim no one can come unless God draws them through a secret, irresistible election. This creates a "chicken and egg" problem where a person must essentially be born again before they can have the faith to be born again. However, John 12:32 destroys this by stating that Christ will "draw **all** men." God is doing a drawing work in every life through the Word and the Spirit; the variable is whether the individual responds or resists.

### The "Inability" Argument Romans 8:7-8 vs. Hebrews 11:6

Reformed doctrine claims "total inability," arguing that man cannot believe because faith is a "work" the carnal mind cannot perform. This is "complete bunk." We see the refutation in the simple faith of a child—like "Liam," a young boy in our congregation who heard the Gospel and responded with "childlike faith." Receiving a free gift is never a "work." Even Rahab the harlot (Hebrews 11), a fleshly woman, heard the Word and believed. Faith is not something God bestows only on a few; it is what happens when any soul hears the Word (Romans 10:17) and stops resisting.

### The "Election" Argument Ephesians 1:4-5

This passage is often twisted to say God chose us "to be in Him." But the text says God "hath chosen us **in him**." This is a "Conditional Election." The election is the corporate blessing and adoption that belongs to the group—the body of Christ. If you are "in Him" through faith, you are part of the chosen; God didn't pick you _to be_ in Him, He picked what happens to those who _are_ in Him.

### The "Ordination" Argument Acts 13:48 vs. Acts 13:46

Calvinists point to verse 48 ("as many as were ordained to eternal life believed") to claim a pre-set decree. They ignore the immediate context in verse 46, where Paul tells the Jews they "put it from [them]" and **"judge [themselves] unworthy of everlasting life."** If people can reject everlasting life and judge themselves unworthy, the "ordination" in verse 48 cannot be an irresistible decree for a few. It is the decree that those who believe—in this case, the Gentiles—are the ones who receive life.

**The "So What?" Layer** These Reformed interpretations "deny the power of God's word" to build faith. By replacing the hearing of the Word with a secret decree, they bypass the biblical mechanism for salvation and leave souls in a state of confusion rather than assurance.

---

## 6. Conclusion: The Pastoral and Ethical Implications of Calvinist Doctrine

The classification of historic Reformed theology as "heresy" and "blasphemy" is necessary because the doctrine limits the very blood of Christ. To say the Saviour did not die for the whole world is to attack the magnitude of His sacrifice. This is not a secondary disagreement; it is an assault on the Gospel.

The ethical crisis of Calvinism is most apparent at the Great White Throne Judgment. In the Calvinist view, a person cast into hell could technically "make a case" against God, saying, "I was decreed incapable of believing in a Saviour who never died for me." This would make God the author of their unbelief. In the Biblical view, however, "every mouth will be stopped." Every individual in hell will know they had a genuine opportunity and willfully rejected it.

**The "So What?" Layer** The call to action is clear: get the Scriptures in front of souls. Faith comes by hearing, not by secret decrees. We must rely on the power of the Word to quicken hearts, trusting that God’s "whosoever will" is a sincere offer to every man, woman, and child. Reject the "double speak" of human tradition and stand on the clear, glorious promises of God's holy Word.
